# "Worth the Wait": Frederick the Great and his greyhounds (Feeloy short, ~25 s)

## Facts (checked 2026-11-15)
- Frederick II of Prussia died at Sanssouci on 17 Aug 1786. He wanted to be buried in a vault on the top vineyard terrace
  at Sanssouci, beside the graves of his greyhounds (Italian greyhounds; the dog vault is there, graves in a row).
- His nephew and successor had him buried in the Garrison Church in Potsdam, next to his father, instead.
- WWII: the coffins were hidden (finally in a salt mine), found by US forces, reburied in St Elisabeth's Church, Marburg;
  moved to Hohenzollern Castle in 1952/53.
- 17 Aug 1991, the 205th anniversary of his death, the coffin lay in state at Sanssouci and he was buried that night in
  the plot he had chosen, next to his dogs (Deseret News 18 Aug 1991; LRB; SPSG / Sanssouci).
- Visitors leave potatoes on his grave (he pushed potato farming in Prussia).
Sources: deseret.com/1991/8/18 (reburial "205 years too late"), lrb.co.uk/v38/n15, spsg.de Sanssouci page,
royalcentral.co.uk (wishes, nephew), unofficialroyalty.com (Marburg, Hohenzollern 1953-1991).
Caveat: "loved his greyhounds more than most people" is a deadpan line; he was famously misanthropic and doted on the dogs.
The script does not name Hitler or the Nazis ("a war moved him to a mine").
