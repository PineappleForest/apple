# "Asking for Company" (Helen Morrison's lonely-hearts ad, Manchester 1727 -> Feeloy), a 20-30 s short

Composition (to build once the take arrives): `AdShort`, code `src/videos/ad/`, output `out/ad_final.mp4`.
Narration: ElevenLabs "Mark". 64 counted words: ~22 s at our usual pace; the video adds the end card (~25 s total).

## Facts (with sources) and how firm they are
- 1727: Helen Morrison placed a personal ad seeking a husband in the Manchester Weekly Journal; the mayor had her committed
  to an asylum for a month. Widely repeated (popular histories, e.g. Laura Schaefer's book on personal ads), but thinly
  sourced -> the script says "So the story goes" and does NOT call it the first personal ad.
  - https://medium.com/timeline/tinder-personal-ads-history-4c34c7d6dbcb
  - https://riskyregencies.com/tag/personal-ads/
- The first known lonely-hearts ad is a man's, 19 July 1695, in John Houghton's London pamphlet (Francesca Beauman,
  *Shapely Ankle Preferr'd*, 2011) - not used in the script, kept here so nobody writes "the first".
  - https://www.futilitycloset.com/2022/06/17/seeking
- Depiction: a Georgian Manchester street + print shop; Helen (bonnet, generic cartoon) handing in her ad; the newspaper
  card with a heart ad; the stern mayor (wig, chain of office) reading it; a barred door + a calendar month flipping; then
  the modern flat with Jenny.

## Script (64 counted words)
1. In seventeen twenty-seven, Helen Morrison of Manchester put an ad in the newspaper.
2. She was looking for a husband.
3. The mayor read it, and had her locked in an asylum for a month. So the story goes.
4. Her crime? Asking for company.
5. Today, that's just called an app.
6. Talk to Jenny on Feeloy. No mayor will mind.
7. Search Feeloy on the App Store.
