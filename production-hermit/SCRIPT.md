## Facts (with sources)
- 18th-century English landowners hired "ornamental"/"garden" hermits to live in a hermitage on their grounds as an
  attraction for guests (a Georgian fashion; historian Gordon Campbell, *The Hermit in the Garden*, OUP 2013).
  - https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/ornamental-hermits-were-18th-century-englands-must-have-garden-accessory-180982469/
- The Hon. Charles Hamilton (1704-86) laid out Painshill, Cobham, Surrey, 1738-1773, with a hermitage. His advert: seven
  years in the hermitage with a Bible, spectacles, a mat, a hassock, an hourglass, water and food from the house; never
  cut hair, beard or nails, never leave the grounds, never speak to the servants. £700 if he lasted the seven years,
  otherwise nothing.
  - https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/500916/short-lived-british-fashion-ornamental-hermits
  - https://www.countrylife.co.uk/gardens/curious-questions-what-is-a-garden-hermit-259843
  - https://www.hermitary.com/lore/ornamental_hermits.html
- A man named Remington took the post; three weeks later he was found drinking in the local pub "so the legend goes"
  (Painshill's own site calls it legend) -> the script says "legend says".
  - https://www.painshill.co.uk/attractions/the-hermitage/
- Hawkstone Park, Shropshire (Richard Hill): the hermit "Father Francis" (1780s) was such an attraction that the Hill
  family built a pub, the Hawkstone Arms, for the visitors; at other hours the hermit was replaced with a mannequin.
  - https://www.mentalfloss.com/history/short-lived-british-fashion-ornamental-hermits
  - https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/the-history-of-hermits-in-gardens.amp
- The garden gnome as the hermit's descendant: Campbell's subtitle ("From Imperial Rome to Ornamental Gnome") and several
  writers make the link; played as a light joke, not a claim.
- Depiction: a Georgian park (lake, temple, grotto); the hermitage = a rustic root-and-thatch hut; the hermit in a
  camlet robe whose hair/beard grow; the advert as a parchment card; the pub with a hanging sign; Hawkstone visitors
  queueing with a bell; a straw dummy on the hermit's stool; a garden gnome on the same stool at the end.
