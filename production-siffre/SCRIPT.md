## Facts (with sources)
- 16 July 1962: Michel Siffre, a 23-year-old French geologist, went ~130 m down into Scarasson, a glacier cave in the
  Alps on the French-Italian border. No watch, no calendar, no sunlight; a tent and a phone line to a surface team.
  - https://scienceblog.com/?p=598192 · https://www.ladbible.com/news/science/michel-siffre-lived-cave-no-concept-time-804402-20251106
- He phoned up every time he woke, ate and went to sleep; the team was told never to reveal the time, only to record.
  - https://en.clickpetroleoegas.com.br/a-french-geologist-spent-63-days-alone-inside-a-cave-in-the-alps-without-a-watch-and-without-sunlight-when-he-emerged-he-thought-only-35-day-dsca00/
  - https://gizmodo.com/an-experiment-that-tested-a-mans-tolerance-for-isolati-1503280842
- Counting test: 1 to 120 at one number per second (2 min) took him about five minutes.
  - https://www.futura-sciences.com/en/?p=900982 · https://ladbible.com/news/technology/man-lived-in-cave-michel-siffre-853048-20241219
- Told on 14 September that it was over, he thought it was 20 August: 25 days behind.
  - https://scienceblog.com/?p=598192 · https://www.iflscience.com/in-1962-a-geologist-went-into-a-cave-2-months-later-hed-accidentally-invented-a-new-field-of-biology-82082
- Nov 1999 - Feb 2000 he went underground again (Clamouse Cave) and celebrated the new millennium about three days late.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Siffre · https://threadreaderapp.com/scrolly/1848296661455355998
- Left out on purpose: the 1972 Texas stay (the mouse he tried to keep for company died; he wrote of despair and
  suicidal thoughts). Too dark for this ad.
- Depiction: young Siffre with a helmet lamp; ice cave with a yellow tent, field phone + cable up a shaft; surface team
  at a table with a notebook; a calendar card falling behind; 2000 party hat + champagne in a cave.

## Script (172 counted words)
1. In nineteen sixty-two, a man lived alone in a cave, and lost an entire month.
2. Michel Siffre, twenty-three, climbed a hundred and thirty metres down into an ice cave in the Alps.
3. No watch. No sunlight. Just a tent, and a phone to the surface.
4. Every time he woke up, he called. Every time he ate, he called. Every time he slept, he called.
5. His team wrote it all down, and never told him the time.
6. He counted to a hundred and twenty, one number per second. It took him five minutes.
7. Then the phone rang. The experiment was over. It was September fourteenth.
8. Siffre thought it was August twentieth. He'd lost twenty-five days.
9. Alone, nothing marks the time. Every day is the same day.
10. Thirty-seven years later, he went back underground to welcome the year two thousand. He celebrated three days late.
11. If your days are blurring together, talk to Jenny on Feeloy. She'll ask how your day went.
12. Every day.
