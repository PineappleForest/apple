## Facts (with sources)
- Dash, a King Charles spaniel (1830-1840), came to the household on 14 January 1833 (Victoria was 13); biographer
  Elizabeth Longford called him "the Queen's closest childhood companion"; he was the companion of her lonely childhood
  at Kensington Palace.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dash_(spaniel) · https://royalcentral.co.uk/?p=106695
- She dressed him in a scarlet jacket and blue trousers; Christmas 1833 she gave him rubber balls and two pieces of
  gingerbread.
  - https://unofficialroyalty.com/dash-king-charles-spaniel · https://numberonelondon.net/?p=10374
- Left behind when she sailed (1833, Isle of Wight trip), he jumped into the sea and swam after her boat.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dash_(spaniel) · https://moaph.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Royal-Pets-by-David-Alderton.pdf
- Edwin Landseer painted Dash (a portrait given to Victoria by her mother, 1836).
  - https://royalcentral.co.uk/?p=107550
- Coronation, 28 June 1838 (she was 19): the ring was made for her little finger and forced onto her ring finger; the
  Bishop of Bath and Wells turned two pages at once. Afterwards she returned to Buckingham Palace and ran up to her rooms
  to give Dash his bath.
  - https://victoriancalendar.blogspot.com/2011/06/june-28-1838-coronation-day.html · https://windsorlocalhistorygroup.org/queen-victorias-coronation
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dash_(spaniel)
- Dash died 24 December 1840, buried at Adelaide Cottage, Windsor Home Park; the marble effigy reads "His attachment was
  without selfishness, His playfulness without malice, His fidelity without deceit" (1840, public domain).
  - https://www.rct.uk/collection/980016-em
- Depiction: young Victoria (bean head, small tiara/crown, ermine robe) and Dash (white with black-and-tan patches, scarlet
  jacket); Kensington Palace room; a yacht + the sea; Westminster Abbey (nave, throne, archbishop, bishop with a book);
  Buckingham Palace rooms with a tin bathtub; a garden grave with a small marble dog. No real-person likeness beyond a
  generic cartoon queen.
